The Assignment Tracker

Last year, flipping between tabs on the curriculum spreadsheets to see both kids' assignments was so painful, and worse - I kept losing track of what we had done or not. So I started copying and pasting my girls' assignments into my notes app, called Notion, one at a time. That was even more painful.

So then, I built a tool.

A few clicks and both kids' assignments landed in a single place where I could edit the plan to what worked for us, track progress, and then mark assignments done and move on with my life.

Since our school just moved to Gradelink, I had to tweak the tool from last year's version. Now it's a Chrome extension and there's no software to install or run. So, consider this a starting point rather than a finished thing, and let me know where it breaks (and bends). If you can't get it going, I'll meet you at the coffee shop and walk you through it. 😉

~ Brianne

⚠️ A note on privacy and security

The extension does not send or save any information that I or anyone else can access. The extension has no server. There is no account to create, nothing is collected, and nothing is sent anywhere except to your own Notion workspace, when you ask it to.

You can read the full privacy policy here.

01

Install the extension

It lives in the Chrome Web Store, which means you can install it in just a few clicks and use it with one click each week, right from your Chrome browser.

  1. Open Gradelink to Notion.
  2. Click Add to Chrome, then Add extension when Chrome asks.
  3. Click the puzzle-piece icon at the top right of Chrome (next to the search bar), find Gradelink to Notion in the list, and click the pin next to it.

That third step is optional, but helpful; pinning puts the button on your toolbar where you can reach it. Chrome tucks new extensions behind that puzzle piece by default, and the button is how you run this every week.

You won't find this extension by searching the Chrome extension store

It's unlisted on purpose, so the link above is the way in. It's still the same store and the same install; it just isn't advertised to strangers at other schools.

02

Set up Notion

Notion is a free app — that can be used for all kinds of things - from my simple assignment tracker, all the way to an all-encompassing life planner, and everything in between.

If you already use it, skip to number 3 in this list.

  1. Make a free account. Go to notion.com, click Get Notion free, and sign up with your email or your Google account. Pick For personal use when it asks. The free plan covers everything here.
  2. Get the app — optional, but nicer than the browser. notion.com/desktop
  3. Open the Notion template — The Assignment Tracker
  4. Click the duplicate icon at the top right — two overlapping squares, just left of the ••• menu. There is no button that says "Duplicate"; the icon is it. If you aren't signed in to Notion yet, it will ask you to first.
  5. It copies into your own Notion workspace. That copy is yours — rename it, change the colors, make it look like your house.
  6. Rename First Child and Second Child to your own children. Open either EXAMPLE row, click the Person label, choose Edit property, then the option you want to rename and type in the box at the top of the menu.
  7. Rename the two boards to match. That's a separate label from the one above: click the settings icon at the far right of a board, next to the blue New button, and type the child's name in the box at the top.
03

Make your Notion key

This is the most technical part of the whole setup, but it's the most important because it's what lets each assignment arrive with its full description — the teacher's bolded heading, the goal, the materials, the instructions, and attachment links.

You're making a key that only opens your own tracker, and handing it to the extension.

  1. Open notion.so/my-integrations in a browser.
  2. Click New integration.
  3. Name it something like Homeschool, pick your workspace, click Submit.
  4. Copy the Internal Integration Secret — a long code starting ntn_ or secret_. Keep it somewhere for a minute; you'll paste it in the next step.
  5. Open your tracker in Notion. Click the ••• menu at the top right, choose Connections, and pick the integration you just made. Without this the key opens nothing — it's the step everybody misses, and it's why the extension can't find your tracker later.
⚠️ Treat that secret like a password

Your Notion key allows access to your personal Notion app; anyone who has it can access whatever you put in Notion. Once you paste it in to the extension, it's kept by the extension on your own computer, and it goes nowhere except to your Notion.

Never send a copy of the key to anyone - including me. If something breaks, send me what the extension says on screen. That's all I need, and it has no secret in it.

04

Fill in the settings

Click the extension button, then Settings.

Connect to Notion

  1. Paste your secret into Integration secret.
  2. Click Find. It lists the trackers that key can reach — usually just the one.
  3. Pick the assignment tracker from the list.
  4. Press Save, then Check connection. It should tell you your tracker is reachable.

If Find comes back empty-handed, it's almost always #5 in the "Make your Notion key" step above — the key exists but hasn't been connected to the tracker yet.

Your children

One row per child. On the left, the name you set up in step #2 for your Notion tracker. On the right, the name as Gradelink spells it (it's the name in the menu at the top right of the portal), because schools often use the full name for a child everyone calls something shorter: Katherine for Katie, Samuel for Sam.

Skipping this section doesn't lose any assignments, but it files them under the name the school uses instead of the name you setup.

Subjects

Course names out of Gradelink might be long and school-ish. This is where you shorten them: when a course name contains the words on the left, the work is filed under the subject on the right.

Whatever you type on the right has to match a Subject option in your tracker (instructions for how to add a subject in Notion are on the template). Anything that doesn't match a subject lands under Other — that's the box underneath, and you can change it.

Courses to skip

Some courses you don't need on a board at all — electives, chapel, study hall. Type a word from the course name here and nothing from that course ever gets sent to Notion.

Personal homeschool additions

The stuff you might add in yourself that isn't in Gradelink — piano practice, handwriting practice, extra reading. Each one gets added every week. The days are optional: set them and each week's copy carries that weekday's date; leave them and the row arrives without a date.

Press Save when you're done. You can come back and change any of this later.

05

Each week

  1. Log into Gradelink the way you normally do.
  2. Click Grades on the left.
  3. Check the dropdown at the top is showing the current quarter.
  4. Click the extension button on your toolbar, then Sync this page to Notion.
That's the whole thing.

You don't have to switch kids in the portal. The extension handles this itself.

06

What it tells you

When it's done, the panel shows you what happened.

Added is the new work, listed per child. Already there is everything it recognized and left alone.

Most weeks it'll say "already done" a lot

Gradelink shows the whole quarter every time, not just this week, so the tool keeps re-reading work it already handled. It remembers and skips it. A short "added" list next to a long "already done" list is it working properly.

Added recently — add it again? Your personal additions repeat every week, so if this week's copy already went in, it holds the item back rather than writing a second one. It tells you when it last went in and gives you a button. It never drops something without telling you. If it decided not to write something, it says so and lets you overrule it.

Ignored courses lists the ones you told it to skip, so you can check the list is right.

Things to look at is the section to actually read. A child it couldn't reach, a name that didn't match — anything it couldn't make sense of ends up here.

07

The boards

The front page of your tracker holds one board per child, and each board shows that child's work in three columns — Not started, In progress, Done. Click the assignment card to open it and see the description. Drag a card across to the "Done" column as your kiddo finishes the assignment.

There's nothing to set up each week

New assignments automatically load onto the boards after you run the sync each week. When you don't need to see an assignment on a board anymore, just check the "archived" box on the assignment. Archiving clears the assignment off the board without losing it, and you can untick the box any time to bring it back.

Under the boards is the full Assignments List table, every row the tool has ever written. You'll rarely need it, but that's where everything lives if you want to search back through the year.

Note

If you delete an assignment, the sync tool remembers it already wrote that one, so it won't come back into Notion the next time you run the sync — including if you deleted it by mistake. Archiving is the reversible version.

When something goes sideways

"Not set up yet"

The settings haven't been filled in. Go back to step 4 — it needs the key and the tracker picked before it can do anything.

"Open your Gradelink Grades page first"

The button was clicked from some other tab. Go to Gradelink, click Grades, and click it from there.

"Nothing came back from the Gradelink page"

Usually a page that's been sitting open long enough to be logged out. Reload it and try again.

"That key works, but it has not been given access to anything yet"

Step 5 of section 3 — the key is real but it hasn't been given the door. Open your tracker in Notion, ••• → Connections, pick your integration, then press Find again.

Notion could not find your tracker

Same cause, usually — the integration got disconnected from the tracker, or you're pointed at a different database than the one you connected. Press Find again and re-pick it.

"Nothing was read for [child]"

The name doesn't match. Open the settings and check the Gradelink spelling for that child against the menu at the top right of the portal.

A course landed under "Other"

It didn't recognize that course name. Nothing's broken, and the assignment is still there — add a subject rule for it in the settings, or send me the course name.

It added way fewer assignments than the page shows

That's expected. Gradelink shows the whole quarter every time, so most of what's on that page went in during previous weeks. Check the "already there" list in the summary. If something's missing from both lists, tell me.

I ran it twice by accident

Nothing bad happens. Anything already in there gets left alone rather than doubled up.

The button doesn't do anything

Check that the extension is still pinned and still enabled — chrome://extensions lists it with a switch. Chrome sometimes turns extensions off after an update.
